How the grade works, and what it can't tell you

Counted seasons. A season counts only if the player was at the club for all of it and still there at the end, however many minutes he played. A summer signing counts from that season. A winter signing (January) counts from the next full season, since the half-season he arrived in is not counted. The current season doesn't count until it ends.

Score. Per 90 minutes, as a percentile against players in the same position group, season and data source. Forwards: expected goals plus assists. Midfielders depend on role. Defensive midfielders (6) are judged like defenders, on defensive output. Central midfielders (8) on how well they control the midfield (passing, territory and recoveries compared with their own team). Attacking midfielders (10) on attacking output like forwards. Goals and assists add bonus marks for 6s and 8s. Forwards and attacking midfielders are half judged against their own team's attacking output. Centre-backs: defensive output only (involvement compared with their own team, duel and aerial win rates), plus bonus points for goals and assists. Full-backs (left- and right-backs): 60% that defensive output and 40% attacking output, with no bonus. Goalkeepers: goals prevented. Seasons under 450 minutes are shown but not scored. Each scored season is pulled toward 50 in proportion to how few minutes it rests on (weight = minutes ÷ (minutes + 300)): 450 minutes keeps 60% of its distance from average, 900 minutes keeps 75%, 3,000 minutes keeps 91%.

Verdicts. Bargain: cost €5m or less and beat the fee's prediction by 15+ points. Great value: 30+ above. Good value: 15 to 30 above. About fair: within 15 either way. Overpriced: 15+ below the prediction but a score of 25 or more. Poor signing: 15+ below and a score under 25.

Fee predicts. A simple regression of score on the log of the fee, with position, year, league and window adjustments, and a separate fee slope for the Premier League section and the other four leagues (the Premier League's fee slope is steeper, and a cross-validation test shows this fits no worse than one shared slope). Fees are first converted to 2025 money using the average paid fee of each year within the same section, so a €30m fee in 2012 is compared with what €30m buys today only after that scaling. Value is the score minus what the fee predicts. Fee explains only about a tenth of the spread, so treat it as a lead, not a verdict.

Range. The shaded band on the score bar and the "likely value range" are 90% ranges. They combine two things: how noisy a season's score is for the minutes behind it (measured from how much players' scores move between consecutive seasons, which also includes real year-to-year change), and how uncertain the fee line is. A "firm" verdict means at least 80% of that range sits inside the verdict's band; below 55% it could go either way.

Limits. Data sources differ by league and year (Opta, FBref, SofaScore) and are never ranked against each other. FBref minutes cover all competitions. All five leagues start in 2017/18, the first season detailed stats (tackles, interceptions, duels, passes, expected goals) exist for all of them; before that only goals, assists and cards are on record, which is not enough to grade defenders or midfielders. Goalkeepers are graded on goals prevented (post-shot expected goals minus goals conceded). Fees are the stated transfer fee only. Players Transfermarkt lists as retired are excluded.
